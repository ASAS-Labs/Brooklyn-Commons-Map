The map

A prior map, not a picture

This cloud is the reference the vehicle matches live LiDAR scans against to work out where it is. Everything about how it is stored is in service of that, not of how it looks.

Local frame, no projection

The map carries projector_type: Local, so coordinates are plain metres in the map's own frame rather than a geographic projection. The lanelet file stores local_x and local_y in that same frame, which is why the route overlays the cloud exactly, with no reprojection anywhere in the viewer.

Anchoring that frame to the world is still open. It costs a click to set the initial pose and nothing else, so it has not blocked anything.

Why it is coloured by height

The obvious choice is reflectivity, and it is the wrong one. A plaza is overwhelmingly flat ground, so a reflectivity ramp lets the floor dominate while tree canopy — which returns weakly — goes black and disappears.

Height over a measured band separates exactly what you are trying to see. On this cloud the 2nd percentile sits at −1.27 m and the 97th at 12.71 m, so the ramp spans −1.3 to 13 m. The viewer still offers reflectivity and baked surface colour as modes.

The vehicle

Measured, derived, or still a guess

The vehicle model started as Autoware's sample Lexus. Its wheelbase was wrong by a factor of 2.2 and its steering limit by 2.4, and both feed the controller directly. Everything below is tagged by what kind of number it actually is.

Steering is asymmetric by 29.2% in tire angle, from two effects that compound: 19.2% in shaft travel and 11.1% in the shaft-to-tire linkage. Autoware accepts one symmetric limit, so it gets the smaller side — a clamp that is too tight only costs turning circle, one that is too loose lets the planner ask for an angle the vehicle cannot reach.
